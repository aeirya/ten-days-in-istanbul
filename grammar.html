THE MAIN IDEA

Turkish builds meaning by stacking suffixes

The stem usually stays recognizable and grammatical information gets attached to the end. Learn the order and the vowel harmony, and long words stop looking random.

noun stem + plural + possessive + case
ev+ ler+ imiz+ den→ evlerimizden = from our houses
verb stem + negation/ability + tense/aspect + person
gel+ me+ di+ m→ gelmedim = I didn’t come

VOWEL HARMONY

Why one suffix appears in several shapes

Two-way harmony: A → a / e

After back vowels a, ı, o, u, use a. After front vowels e, i, ö, ü, use e.

evler, arabalar, evde, otelde

Four-way harmony: I → ı / i / u / ü

The vowel follows both front/back and rounded/unrounded features of the previous vowel.

kapıyı, evi, yolu, günü

Capital A and I are common shorthand for “choose the harmonizing vowel,” not literal Turkish letters.

SOUND CHANGES AT SUFFIX BOUNDARIES

The stem can change a little

p / ç / t / k may soften before a vowel

kitap → kitabı, ağaç → ağacı, kanat → kanadı, çocuk → çocuğu.

Not every word does this, so learn common words as you meet them.

d can become t after a voiceless consonant

Locative/ablative suffixes alternate: evde but parkta; evden but parktan.

VERBS

Start with the stem

Dictionary forms end in -mak / -mek. Remove that to get the stem: gelmek → gel-, gitmek → git-, almak → al-, istemek → iste-, çalışmak → çalış-.

MeaningPatternExample
Present progressive-(I)yor + persongeliyorum = I’m coming · çalışıyor = it is working
Simple past-DI + persongeldim = I came · gittik = we went
Future-(y)AcAk + persongeleceğim = I will come · gideceğiz = we will go

QUESTIONS + REQUESTS

The forms you will use constantly

Yes/no question particle: mı / mi / mu / mü

Written separately: Çalışıyor mu? “Does it work?” · Geldiniz mi? “Did you come?”

Can I…? = -(y)Abil + question

Alabilir miyim? “Can I get/buy it?” · Bakabilir misiniz? “Could you look?”

Imperative

Informal: gel “come,” bekle “wait.” Polite/plural: gelin, bekleyin.

Negative imperative

gelme “don’t come” · dokunmayın “please don’t touch.”

NOUN/ADJECTIVE SENTENCES

Turkish often needs no separate word for “is”

Predicate

Pahalı. = It is expensive. · Hazırım. = I am ready. · Yorgunum. = I am tired.

Negative with değil

Pahalı değil. = It isn’t expensive. · Hazır değilim. = I’m not ready.

Existence: var / yok

Wi-Fi var mı? = Is there Wi-Fi? · Yok. = There isn’t / no.

Question words stay in place

Bu ne kadar? = How much is this? · Metro nerede? = Where is the metro?
