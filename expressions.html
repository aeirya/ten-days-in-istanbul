START WITH THESE

The ten you will use all day

MerhabaHelloNeutral and safe everywhere.
LütfenPleaseUseful, but Turkish often sounds natural without adding it to every request.
TeşekkürlerThanksEasy, neutral.
Sağ olThanksVery common and informal. Literally “be healthy.”
Sağ olunThank youPolite or plural version of sağ ol.
PardonExcuse me / sorryQuickest way to get past someone or correct a small bump.
TamamOkay / all rightExtremely common.
GörüşürüzSee youThe easiest general goodbye.
Kolay gelsinHope the work goes easilySay it to someone who is working.
Afiyet olsunEnjoy your mealBefore, during, or after someone eats. Reply: Teşekkür ederim / Sağ olun.

THANKS + RESPONSES

Turkish “دستت درد نکنه”: learn these as a family

Persian دستت درد نکنه does not map to one fixed Turkish phrase. Pick the formula by what you are thanking someone for:

TurkishClosest senseUse
Eline sağlık / Ellerine sağlıkدستت درد نکنهInformal: especially for something someone cooked, made, or did with their hands. Ellerine sağlık is the very common “health to your hands” form.
Elinize sağlık / Ellerinize sağlıkدستتون درد نکنهPolite/plural version; excellent when thanking a cook or restaurant owner for food.
Emeğine sağlık / Emeğinize sağlıkخسته نباشی / ممنون بابت زحمتتThanks for someone’s effort or work, not specifically their hands.
Sağ ol / Sağ olunمرسی / دمت گرم / دستت درد نکنهGeneral-purpose thanks. Sağ olun is polite or plural.
Teşekkür ederimمتشکرمPolite, neutral, slightly fuller.
Çok teşekkürlerخیلی ممنونNeutral.
Sağ olasınدمت گرم / ممنونWarm, informal, a little more expressive than sağ ol.
When someone thanks you: Teşekkür ederim / Sağ ol(un) → Rica ederim (standard), Bir şey değil (“it’s nothing”), or Ne demek (“don’t mention it”). Learn the thanks and its replies together.
Restaurant example: If the owner keeps checking on you and taking care of your table, Her şey için çok teşekkür ederiz. Çok ilgilendiniz, sağ olun. is more natural than elinize sağlık. Use elinize/ellerinize sağlık when praising the food or something they made.
Don’t mix these up: sağ olasın is said to someone; sağ olsun is usually about a third person: “bless him/her / thanks to him/her.”

EXCUSE ME + GETTING ATTENTION

How to interrupt without sounding abrupt

PardonExcuse me / sorryShortest and easiest.
AffedersinizExcuse meMore polite/formal; good with strangers.
Kusura bakmayınSorry / excuse meFor an actual inconvenience or minor mistake.
Bakar mısınız?Excuse me / could you look?Common for getting a waiter/shop worker’s attention.
Bir saniyeOne second“Hold on a second.”
Buyurun?Yes? / go ahead?What someone may say when you approach or call them.
