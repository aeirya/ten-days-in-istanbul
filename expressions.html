START WITH THESE

The ten you will use all day

MerhabaHelloNeutral and safe everywhere.
LütfenPleaseUseful, but Turkish often sounds natural without adding it to every request.
TeşekkürlerThanksEasy, neutral.
Sağ olThanksVery common and informal. Literally “be healthy.”
Sağ olunThank youPolite or plural version of sağ ol.
PardonExcuse me / sorryQuickest way to get past someone or correct a small bump.
TamamOkay / all rightExtremely common.
GörüşürüzSee youThe easiest general goodbye.
Kolay gelsinHope the work goes easilySay it to someone who is working.
Afiyet olsunEnjoy your mealBefore, during, or after someone eats.

THANKS + RESPONSES

More natural than repeating teşekkür ederim

TurkishMeaningUse
Teşekkür ederimThank youPolite, neutral, slightly fuller.
Çok teşekkürlerThanks a lotNeutral.
Sağ olThanksInformal singular; extremely useful.
Sağ olunThank youPolite singular or plural.
Sağ olasınThanks / bless youWarm, informal, a little more expressive than sağ ol.
Rica ederimYou’re welcomeStandard polite reply.
Bir şey değilIt’s nothingCasual “no problem.”
Ne demekDon’t mention itWarm response to thanks.
Don’t mix these up: sağ olasın is said to someone; sağ olsun is usually about a third person: “bless him/her / thanks to him/her.”

EXCUSE ME + GETTING ATTENTION

How to interrupt without sounding abrupt

PardonExcuse me / sorryShortest and easiest.
AffedersinizExcuse meMore polite/formal; good with strangers.
Kusura bakmayınSorry / excuse meFor an actual inconvenience or minor mistake.
Bakar mısınız?Excuse me / could you look?Common for getting a waiter/shop worker’s attention.
Bir saniyeOne second“Hold on a second.”
Buyurun?Yes? / go ahead?What someone may say when you approach or call them.

HELLO + GOODBYE

The goodbye pair that is worth learning correctly

TurkishMeaningWho says it?
Merhaba / SelamHello / hiSelam is more casual.
GünaydınGood morningAnyone.
İyi akşamlarGood eveningAlso works when leaving in the evening.
GörüşürüzSee youEither person; safest everyday goodbye.
Hoşça kalGoodbye / stay wellThe person leaving says it to the person staying.
Güle güleGoodbye / go happilyThe person staying says it to the person leaving.
İyi gecelerGood nightLate evening / before sleep.
Kendine iyi bakTake careInformal singular.

VERY TURKISH SOCIAL FORMULAS

These make you sound much more locally aware

Kolay gelsinMay the work come easilyTo a cashier, repair person, cleaner, driver, shop worker — anyone visibly working.
Afiyet olsunEnjoy your mealTo someone eating; also heard from restaurant staff.
Eline sağlıkHealth to your handsInformal praise/thanks to the person who cooked or made something.
Elinize sağlıkHealth to your handsPolite/plural version.
Geçmiş olsunHope it passes / get well soonIllness, injury, accident, or any unpleasant event.
Hayırlı olsunMay it be auspiciousFor a new purchase, job, home, business, etc.
Hoş geldinizWelcomePolite/plural. Reply: Hoş bulduk.
Çok yaşaBless youAfter a sneeze. A common reply is Sen de gör.
